What the world is covering

News from thousands of outlets in dozens of languages, grouped into stories and pinned where they happen. Every pin shows its sources and how far to trust it.

How to read it. Every article is grouped with the others covering the same story. A pin’s size is its signal: how many different headlines the story has. Outlets that run the identical wire copy count once, and the outlet count is shown beside it. Confidence: high = three or more different headlines; medium = two; low = one, so verify. Non-English headlines are shown in their original language. A pin sits where most of the coverage places the story; the card says how that was decided, and place tagging is imperfect.

Where it comes from. The GDELT Project’s open feed of worldwide news (refreshed every 15 minutes), about sixty named newsroom feeds, and the Hacker News front page. Each is read by a script on the server, never by your browser.

How live is “live”? The data file is rebuilt about hourly from the sources above, and this page checks for a newer copy every five minutes while it is open. It is a fast refresh, not a real-time stream.

No cookies, no trackers. This page loads one data file and nothing else leaves your browser. To mark what is new, your browser keeps a short list of story ids you have already seen, on this device only.
