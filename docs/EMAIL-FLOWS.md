# Email flows

Email is where this business actually becomes profitable. This audience opens
email at 25–35% — roughly triple what a general consumer list does — and it
costs nothing to send.

**Tool:** MailerLite free tier (up to 1,000 subscribers, automations included).
Klaviyo is better but its free tier caps at 250 and it gets expensive fast.
Start with MailerLite; move at ~800 subscribers if it's working.

**Voice:** write like a person, not a brand. No exclamation marks stacked up,
no "Hey girl," no countdown timers. Short sentences. Signed by a name.

---

## Flow 1 — Post-purchase (every buyer, automatic)

The point is to reduce refunds and produce reviews. It is not a sales flow.

**Day 0 — Order confirmation**
> Subject: Your Everly Grace order is on its way
>
> Thank you — genuinely. We're a very small operation and every order matters here.
>
> Your set is packed and heading out from our U.S. warehouse. You'll get tracking
> within a day or two, and it usually arrives in three to six days.
>
> One thing worth knowing before it lands: it takes about ten seconds to fit the
> first one, and it's easier than it looks. Instructions are in the box, and if
> anything is unclear just reply to this email — it comes straight to me.

**Day 3 — The setup email (this is the refund-prevention email)**
> Subject: A short note on fitting them
>
> Your set should be close now. Two things that make the first one easier:
>
> **Start with your simplest chain.** Not the heirloom. Get the feel of it on
> something ordinary first.
>
> **Open the little ring sideways, not outwards.** Twist it open rather than
> pulling it apart — that's how jewelers do it and it won't distort.
>
> If a piece feels too heavy for one converter, use two — one on each side. It
> balances the weight and holds much better. Just ask and I'll send you a second
> set at no charge.

**Day 14 — The review request (your most valuable email)**
> Subject: Did it work?
>
> You've had your set a couple of weeks now. I'd really like to know how it went
> — including if it didn't.
>
> If it worked: would you tell me which necklace you started wearing again? I'd
> like to put a few real customer notes on the site, and I'd rather have three
> true ones than thirty invented ones.
>
> If it didn't work: reply and I'll refund you today. You keep the set. No
> explanation needed.

Send this manually for the first 30 customers if you can. A personally-typed
version gets four or five times the reply rate, and early reviews compound.

**Day 45 — Second purchase**
> Subject: One for someone else?
>
> Most people who buy these end up wanting a second set — usually for a sister,
> a mother, or a friend who mentioned the same thing.
>
> Here's $6 off a second set if that's you: [LINK]
>
> And if not, no need to do anything. I won't send another of these.

## Flow 2 — Abandoned checkout

Stripe Payment Links can send these natively — turn it on in the Stripe
dashboard, it costs nothing and typically recovers 5–10% of abandons.

**1 hour later**
> Subject: You left something behind
>
> No pressure — but you were partway through ordering a set of clasp converters
> and something interrupted you. Here's the link back: [LINK]
>
> If something on the page put you off, I'd honestly like to know what. Just reply.

**24 hours later**
> Subject: The two questions people ask
>
> Before you decide, the two things people usually want to know:
>
> **Will it hold?** They hold well past the weight of an ordinary chain and
> pendant. For a heavy statement piece, use two.
>
> **Do I have to alter my necklace?** No. Nothing is cut or removed — it clips
> onto the clasp that's already there and comes off whenever you like.
>
> And if it isn't right, you have 60 days and you don't have to send it back: [LINK]

## Flow 3 — Welcome (non-buyers who gave an email)

Add a simple email capture to the site later — "$5 off your first order."
Then:

**Immediately** — deliver the code, say what the product does in three lines.
**Day 2** — the drawer story. No selling. Just the observation.
**Day 5** — the "don't buy this if" email. Same trust logic as the video.
**Day 9** — last reminder on the code, then stop.

Four emails, then leave them alone until you have a real reason to write.
This audience will unsubscribe from over-mailing faster than any other, and
they don't come back.

---

## What not to do

- **No fake urgency.** "Only 3 left!" on a dropshipped product is a lie, and
  this audience detects it and resents it more than younger buyers do.
- **No fake reviews, ever.** 16 CFR Part 465 — the FTC rule in force since
  October 2024 — carries civil penalties per violation. It is also just
  dishonest, and the whole brand position here is being trustworthy.
- **No daily emails.** Two a week maximum outside of automated flows.
- **Never buy an email list.** It will burn your sending domain permanently.
