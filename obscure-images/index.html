How the obscurity rating works
  1. Where images come from. Random files from Wikimedia Commons (roughly 100 million freely licensed images) and from the Internet Archive (licensed images only). Most of it has barely been looked at, which is the point.
  2. Internet Archive counts are all-time downloads, which is the nearest thing the Archive publishes to views. Its thumbnails are small, and Saved can’t read their colours.
  3. How “seen” is counted. Wikimedia publishes how many times each file was requested by people (bots excluded) every month since January 2015. We add that up, then add the views made on this site from your browser.
  4. The score. 100 − 20 × log₁₀(1 + views), clamped to 0–100. Zero views is 100, 100 views is about 60, 100,000 views is 0. Tiers: Ghost ≤4 views, Rare ≤30, Quiet ≤300, Familiar ≤3,000, Everywhere above that.
  5. What it can’t see. A picture may have been seen plenty somewhere else on the internet. Treat the number as a floor on how seen it is, not the whole truth.
  6. Licenses. Every image has its own license, shown on its page. Check it, and credit the author, before using anything in commercial work.
